# Beyond the Firmament — Editorial Rules

**Draft v0.1**

These rules govern editorial approval, canon status, and authorized use of the **Beyond the Firmament** universe.

## 1. Purpose

Beyond the Firmament is a collaborative universe, but it is not an unrestricted shared universe.

Contributors are encouraged to create stories, characters, technologies, institutions, cultures, artwork, games, philosophical explorations, and other works. Editorial review exists to preserve a coherent universe in which those contributions can coexist.

The primary editorial objective is **continuity and integrity of canon**, not maximizing the number of accepted works.

## 2. Editorial authority

The **Canon Editor** has final authority over:

- what is accepted into Core Canon;
- what is accepted into Expanded Canon;
- whether a work is designated Canon or Authorized Non-Canon;
- whether a proposed work is consistent with the universe;
- whether use of Beyond the Firmament intellectual property is authorized;
- resolution of conflicts between accepted works;
- revisions needed to preserve continuity.

Community discussion, expert review, and contributor feedback are encouraged, but they do not replace final editorial approval.

## 3. Two publication designations

### Canon

A work or contribution that has been expressly reviewed and accepted as part of the official history, setting, or body of facts of Beyond the Firmament.

Canon works may establish events, people, places, technologies, institutions, terminology, and other facts that later contributors are expected to respect.

### Authorized Non-Canon

A work that has permission to use the Beyond the Firmament universe but is **not** part of official history.

Authorized Non-Canon works may explore alternatives, unresolved possibilities, experimental interpretations, games, or stories that do not fit cleanly into established continuity.

Authorization does not imply canon status.

## 4. No unauthorized public use

Public distribution or commercial use of the Beyond the Firmament universe, name, setting, distinctive characters, terminology, institutions, or other protected project elements requires authorization.

Commercial use requires a separate written license. That license may include a royalty, flat fee, revenue share, no fee, or other terms depending on the project.

Editorial approval and commercial licensing are separate decisions.

## 5. Standards for Canon

A proposed canon contribution should:

- be consistent with established Core Canon;
- avoid unnecessary contradictions with accepted Expanded Canon;
- respect the physical and technological rules of the setting;
- distinguish established science from speculative technology;
- give powerful technologies meaningful limitations, costs, or consequences;
- preserve room for future contributors;
- avoid resolving major philosophical, political, religious, or cultural questions by editorial decree when those questions are better explored through stories;
- contribute something useful, interesting, or necessary to the universe;
- avoid copying protected material from other fictional universes.

Scientific realism is valued, but the setting may contain clearly identified speculative technologies when they serve the universe and remain internally consistent.

## 6. Core Canon versus Expanded Canon

### Core Canon

Core Canon contains foundational facts that define the universe. Changes should be rare and made only when they materially improve the project.

Examples include:

- the broad circumstances on Earth around 2100;
- the founding population;
- the Peregrine and its fundamental technologies;
- major physical rules of the worldship;
- major voyage milestones;
- foundational facts that many other works depend upon.

### Expanded Canon

Expanded Canon contains accepted details that develop the universe without redefining its foundations.

Examples may include:

- individuals and families;
- neighborhoods and settlements;
- historical incidents;
- institutions;
- customs and traditions;
- scientific developments;
- political movements;
- specific adventures during the voyage.

## 7. Stories and canon

A story may be Canon without every statement made by its characters being factually true.

Characters may misunderstand history, lie, disagree, speculate, hold prejudices, or interpret events differently. Canon establishes that the story and its events occurred; it does not necessarily endorse every character's interpretation.

This distinction is important because Beyond the Firmament should allow multiple perspectives on freedom, authority, religion, ethics, governance, technology, and the founders' legacy.

## 8. Avoid over-defining the universe

Contributors should not fill every gap simply because a gap exists.

Some uncertainty is intentional. Important eras, events, institutions, and questions should retain enough open space for future stories.

A proposal that unnecessarily closes off a large area of future storytelling may be rejected even if it is internally consistent.

## 9. Changes to existing canon

Canon can be revised when necessary, but continuity changes should be made cautiously.

When possible:

1. preserve previously accepted work;
2. reconcile apparent conflicts through interpretation or additional context;
3. revise minor details rather than major foundations;
4. use a direct retcon only when the benefit clearly outweighs the disruption.

The Canon Editor may declare older material superseded when necessary.

## 10. Submission and approval

Discussion does not create canon.

A typical path is:

1. idea or proposal;
2. discussion and research;
3. draft;
4. pull request;
5. editorial review and revision;
6. explicit approval;
7. merge into the appropriate canon location.

**Merging into a canon folder is the formal act that makes material canon.**

Authorization for a standalone story, game, artwork, or other project should be recorded separately when appropriate.

## 11. Editorial independence

Popularity does not determine canon.

A proposal may have broad community support and still be rejected if it damages continuity, closes too much creative territory, weakens the setting, or conflicts with the project's direction.

Likewise, an unconventional proposal may be accepted if it strengthens the universe and can be integrated coherently.

## 12. Editorial identity

Editorial decisions may be issued under the Canon Editor's chosen professional name or pseudonym. The public editorial identity does not alter the legal ownership or licensing arrangements of the project.

## 13. Relationship to the Contributor Agreement

These Editorial Rules govern creative and continuity decisions.

The `CONTRIBUTOR_AGREEMENT.md` governs ownership, permissions, licensing, and contributor rights.

If a legal or licensing question conflicts with an editorial rule, the applicable written legal agreement controls.

---

These rules are expected to evolve as the project grows, but changes should preserve the central principle: **Beyond the Firmament is open to creative contribution while maintaining one coherent official universe.**
